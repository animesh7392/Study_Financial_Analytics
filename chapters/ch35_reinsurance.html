Chapter 35: Reinsurance and Alternative Risk Transfer

1. Learning Objectives

  • Understand the strategic, operational, and financial rationale for reinsurance (e.g., capital relief, capacity, variance reduction).
  • Differentiate structurally and operationally between Treaty and Facultative reinsurance, and Proportional (Quota Share, Surplus) versus Non-Proportional (Excess of Loss, Stop-Loss).
  • Master pricing methodologies for Excess of Loss (XL) treaties including Experience Rating (Burning Cost) and Exposure Rating (ILFs).
  • Evaluate the impact of alternative risk transfer (ART) mechanisms such as catastrophe bonds and Industry Loss Warranties (ILWs).
  • Formulate the mathematical optimality of reinsurance using Arrow’s theorem and risk measure minimization.
  • Analyze the nuances of the Indian reinsurance market, particularly the mandatory cessions to GIC Re and operations of foreign branches.

3. Intuition & Motivation

Imagine an insurer, "SafeGuard Gen", writing homeowner policies in coastal Florida or cyclone-prone Odisha (India). SafeGuard collects steady premiums and pays out attritional, day-to-day losses efficiently. However, in the event of a Category 5 hurricane or a severe super-cyclone, the correlated nature of the claims would cause the aggregate loss to skyrocket past the insurer’s total capital reserves. If SafeGuard goes bankrupt, thousands of policyholders are left destitute.

Reinsurance is the "insurance of insurance." By transferring a portion of the risk to a reinsurer—often a global entity with highly diversified exposures—SafeGuard protects its solvency, smooths its earnings volatility, and frees up statutory capital to write new business. It transforms an unbearable heavy-tailed risk into a manageable fixed cost (the reinsurance premium).

4. Formal Theory

4.1 Treaty vs. Facultative

Risk transfer occurs primarily via two modes:

  • Facultative Reinsurance: Negotiated on a per-risk (individual policy) basis. Often used for exceptionally large, complex, or unusual risks (e.g., a multi-billion-dollar oil rig). The reinsurer retains the "faculty" (option) to accept or decline.
  • Treaty Reinsurance: A portfolio-level agreement where the reinsurer automatically accepts a specified portion of a defined class of business. Highly efficient and obligatory for both parties once signed.

4.2 Proportional Reinsurance

The insurer and reinsurer share premiums and losses proportionally. The reinsurer pays the insurer a Ceding Commission to compensate for the insurer's original acquisition and administrative costs.

Definition 35.1: Quota Share (QS) Treaty
A treaty where a fixed percentage \(\alpha\) (where \(0 < \alpha < 1\)) of every risk is ceded. \[ \text{Retained Loss} = (1-\alpha) X, \quad \text{Ceded Loss} = \alpha X \] \[ \text{Retained Premium} = (1-\alpha) P, \quad \text{Ceded Premium} = \alpha P \]
Definition 35.2: Surplus Share Treaty
The retention is defined as a fixed absolute currency amount \(R\) per risk (a "line"). The proportion ceded depends on the Sum Insured (\(SI\)) of each risk. If \(SI \le R\), the insurer retains 100%. If \(SI > R\), the ceded proportion is \(\alpha = \frac{SI - R}{SI}\).

4.3 Non-Proportional Reinsurance (Excess of Loss)

The reinsurer only responds when a loss exceeds a predetermined deductible, called the attachment point or priority (\(D\)), up to a specified limit (\(L\)).

Definition 35.3: Excess of Loss (XL)
For a loss random variable \(X\), the ceded loss to an XL layer with deductible \(D\) and limit \(L\) is: \[ X_{ceded} = \min(\max(X - D, 0), L) \] The retained loss is \( X_{ret} = X - X_{ceded} \).

This is further segmented into:

  • Per Risk XL: Applies to individual risks (e.g., one building burning down).
  • Catastrophe (Per Occurrence) XL: Applies to the aggregate losses from a single event (e.g., a hurricane damaging 5,000 homes).
  • Stop-Loss (Aggregate XL): Applies to the total annual aggregated losses of the insurer.

4.4 Pricing XL Treaties

Pricing typically relies on Experience Rating (using historical data, also known as the Burning Cost method) and Exposure Rating (using industry standard curves like Increased Limits Factors).

Formula 35.1: Burning Cost Rate
\[ \text{Burning Cost Rate} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^n \text{Trended Layer Losses}_t}{\sum_{t=1}^n \text{On-Level Premium}_t \text{ or GNPI}_t} \] The final rate charged is often loaded: \[ \text{Rate Charged} = \frac{\text{Burning Cost Rate} + \text{Fixed Costs}}{1 - \text{Profit Margin} - \text{Brokerage}} \] *GNPI stands for Gross Net Premium Income.

4.5 Alternative Risk Transfer (ART)

ART instruments provide reinsurance-like protection funded directly by capital markets.

  • Catastrophe Bonds (Cat Bonds): Debt instruments where the principal is forgiven if a specific catastrophe trigger (e.g., parametric earthquake magnitude, or modeled industry loss) is met.
  • Industry Loss Warranties (ILWs): Contracts where payout occurs if total industry losses exceed a specified threshold, rather than the insurer's actual losses.

4.6 Optimality of Reinsurance

Theorem 35.1: Arrow's Theorem for Reinsurance (1963)
Assume an insurer seeks to maximize expected utility of wealth (with a risk-averse utility function) and the reinsurance premium is calculated purely on expected ceded loss plus a constant proportional loading. Then, the optimal reinsurance contract that maximizes expected utility, subject to a fixed premium budget, is a Stop-Loss (Aggregate Excess of Loss) contract.

5. Worked Examples

Example 35.1: Quota Share Treaty Economics
Problem: SafeGuard Gen writes a portfolio of motor insurance with a total Gross Written Premium (GWP) of INR 1,000,000,000. They enter a 40% Quota Share treaty. The reinsurer pays a 25% ceding commission. Gross claims for the year are INR 750,000,000. Calculate the net cash flows and underwriting profit for the insurer and reinsurer, ignoring expenses.

Solution:
1. Ceded Premium: \( 40\% \times 1,000,000,000 = \text{INR } 400,000,000 \)
2. Retained Premium: \( 60\% \times 1,000,000,000 = \text{INR } 600,000,000 \)
3. Ceding Commission received by Insurer: \( 25\% \times 400,000,000 = \text{INR } 100,000,000 \)
4. Gross Claims: INR 750,000,000
5. Ceded Claims (paid by reinsurer): \( 40\% \times 750,000,000 = \text{INR } 300,000,000 \)
6. Retained Claims: \( 60\% \times 750,000,000 = \text{INR } 450,000,000 \)

Insurer's Underwriting Result:
Retained Premium + Ceding Comm. - Retained Claims = \( 600m + 100m - 450m = \text{INR } 250,000,000 \).
Reinsurer's Underwriting Result:
Ceded Premium - Ceding Comm. - Ceded Claims = \( 400m - 100m - 300m = \text{INR } 0 \). (Break-even).
Example 35.2: Surplus Share Mechanics
Problem: An insurer has a 3-line surplus treaty with a retention of $1,000,000. This means the treaty provides capacity up to 3 times the retention ($3M) on top of the retention. The total capacity is $4,000,000. Determine the retained and ceded percentages and amounts for three risks with sums insured: (A) $800,000, (B) $2,000,000, (C) $5,000,000. Risk B suffers a loss of $500,000. How much does the reinsurer pay?

Solution:
Risk A (SI = $800,000):
Since SI < Retention, Retained = 100%. Ceded = 0%.

Risk B (SI = $2,000,000):
Retention = $1,000,000 (50%).
Surplus (Ceded) = $2,000,000 - $1,000,000 = $1,000,000 (50%).
For a loss of $500,000 on B, the reinsurer pays 50% = $250,000.

Risk C (SI = $5,000,000):
Retention = $1,000,000.
Treaty limit is 3 lines = $3,000,000.
The remaining $1,000,000 exceeds treaty capacity and must be placed facultatively or retained net (if allowed). The treaty takes $3M / $5M = 60%.
Example 35.3: Burning Cost XL Pricing
Problem: Pricing a $5M xs $5M Per Occurrence treaty. Over the last 3 years, the adjusted GNPI and trended losses > $5M are:
Year 1: GNPI $40M, Losses: $7M, $12M
Year 2: GNPI $45M, Losses: $6M
Year 3: GNPI $50M, Losses: None
Loading factor is 100/80 (i.e., pure premium is divided by 0.8). Calculate the Rate on Line (RoL).

Solution:
Apply layer logic \(\min(\max(X - 5M, 0), 5M)\) to each loss:
Y1: $7M loss \(\rightarrow\) $2M to layer. $12M loss \(\rightarrow\) layer is exhausted, $5M to layer. Total Y1 = $7M.
Y2: $6M loss \(\rightarrow\) $1M to layer.
Y3: $0 to layer.
Total layer losses = $8M.
Total GNPI = 40 + 45 + 50 = $135M.
Burning Cost Rate (Pure Premium Rate) = \( 8 / 135 = 5.925\% \).
Loaded Rate = \( 5.925\% / 0.8 = 7.407\% \).
Estimated Treaty Premium for Year 4 (assuming GNPI $55M) = \( 7.407\% \times 55M = \$4,073,850 \).
Rate on Line (Premium / Limit) = \( \$4.07M / \$5M = 81.4\% \).
Example 35.4: Cat Bond Pricing
Problem: A $100M parametric Cat Bond triggers a 100% principal wipeout if an earthquake of magnitude > 7.5 occurs in a specific zone. Modeler estimates the annual probability of attachment (trigger) is 1.2% (Expected Loss = 1.2%). The risk-free rate is 4%. If investors demand a spread of 4.5% over the risk-free rate, what is the expected return to the investor?

Solution:
Coupon Yield = Risk-Free + Spread = \( 4\% + 4.5\% = 8.5\% \).
Total payout to investor if NO trigger = 108.5% of principal.
Total payout if trigger = 8.5% (assuming coupon is paid before wipeout, though often it is lost too. Let's assume standard loss of principal only at year end).
Expected payout = \( 0.988 \times 108.5 + 0.012 \times 8.5 = 107.198 + 0.102 = 107.3\% \).
Expected return = 7.3%.
The investor's risk premium over risk-free rate is \( 7.3\% - 4\% = 3.3\% \).
Example 35.5: Variance Reduction (Reinsurance Optimization)
Problem: Aggregate loss \( S \sim \text{Normal}(\mu=100, \sigma=40) \). An insurer considers a 50% Quota Share (QS) vs an Aggregate XL (Stop-Loss) treaty that covers \( S > 100 \) up to infinity. Both cost a pure premium equivalent. Compare the variance of the retained risk.

Solution:
Original Variance: \( Var(S) = 40^2 = 1600 \).
1. 50% Quota Share:
Retained loss \( R_{QS} = 0.5 S \).
\( Var(R_{QS}) = (0.5)^2 Var(S) = 0.25 \times 1600 = 400 \).
2. Stop-Loss (deductible = 100 = \mu):
Retained loss \( R_{SL} = \min(S, 100) \).
This heavily truncates the right tail. Calculating variance of a truncated normal requires integration (or tabular lookups). For a standard normal \( Z \), variance of \( \min(Z, 0) \) is \( 1/2 - 1/(2\pi) \approx 0.34 \).
Thus \( Var(R_{SL}) \approx 40^2 \times 0.34 = 545 \).
Note: To make pure premiums equal, the QS premium is 50, whereas the pure premium for SL at the mean is \( \sigma / \sqrt{2\pi} \approx 15.9 \). For exactly equal premium spent, the Stop-Loss deductible would be much lower, drastically reducing retained variance below that of the QS, validating Arrow's Theorem.

6. Python Code

This script calculates the Burning Cost for a given Excess of Loss treaty.


import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

def calculate_burning_cost(gnpi_history, loss_history, deductible, limit, loading_factor):
    """
    Calculate XL Treaty Rate on Line and Estimated Premium based on Burning Cost.
    
    gnpi_history: list of floats (historical GNPI)
    loss_history: list of lists of floats (losses in each historical year)
    deductible: float
    limit: float
    loading_factor: float (e.g., 100/80 = 1.25)
    """
    total_gnpi = sum(gnpi_history)
    layer_losses = []
    
    for losses in loss_history:
        year_layer_loss = 0
        for loss in losses:
            # Apply layer logic: min(max(loss - deductible, 0), limit)
            layer_loss = min(max(loss - deductible, 0), limit)
            year_layer_loss += layer_loss
        layer_losses.append(year_layer_loss)
        
    total_layer_loss = sum(layer_losses)
    
    # Calculate rates
    pure_rate = total_layer_loss / total_gnpi
    loaded_rate = pure_rate * loading_factor
    
    return {
        "Total GNPI": total_gnpi,
        "Total Layer Loss": total_layer_loss,
        "Pure BC Rate": round(pure_rate, 4),
        "Loaded Rate": round(loaded_rate, 4),
        "Rate on Line (RoL)": round(loaded_rate * np.mean(gnpi_history) / limit, 4)
    }

# Data from Example 35.3
gnpi = [40, 45, 50]
losses = [[7, 12], [6], []]

results = calculate_burning_cost(gnpi, losses, deductible=5, limit=5, loading_factor=1.25)
for k, v in results.items():
    print(f"{k}: {v}")

Figure 35.1: Proportional vs Non-Proportional Sharing
A side-by-side bar chart showing a total risk exposure. On the left (Quota Share), a vertical split runs all the way up, demonstrating that the reinsurer shares in attritional, large, and catastrophic losses equally. On the right (Excess of Loss), a horizontal line represents the attachment point. The area below remains solid blue (insurer's retention), and the block above is shaded red (reinsurer's liability up to the limit).

Figure 35.2: The Reinsurance Tower
A classic stack diagram showing how an insurer builds catastrophe protection. The bottom layer is the "Retention" (e.g., $0 to $10M). Above it sits "First Layer XL" ($10M xs $10M). Above that is "Second Layer XL" ($30M xs $20M). The peak might be a Cat Bond layer. This illustrates how different capacities and pricing are layered to reach the 1-in-250 year PML (Probable Maximum Loss).

8. Common Mistakes & Misconceptions

Warning: "Free" Capital vs. Cost of Capital
A common misconception is that reinsurance automatically increases profitability by allowing more volume. Reinsurance always carries a cost (the reinsurer's profit margin). It reduces Expected Return in exchange for lower Volatility (Variance) and lower Capital Requirements. It is an arbitrage of the Cost of Capital between the primary insurer and the global reinsurer.
Warning: Limit Exhaustion
In XL treaties, students often forget that treaties have an Annual Aggregate Limit or a reinstatement provision. If a treaty covers "$5M xs $5M", and one $10M loss occurs, the layer is exhausted. If a second $10M loss occurs, the primary insurer bears it entirely unless the treaty includes a "Reinstatement" clause (usually requiring an additional reinstatement premium).

9. Industry Application Box

Industry Context: Indian Reinsurance Landscape & Mandatory Cessions
In the Indian market, regulatory structure significantly shapes reinsurance strategy. The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) mandates that all primary insurers cede a fixed percentage (historically between 4% and 5%, recently adjusted based on class) of their domestic business to the national reinsurer, GIC Re (General Insurance Corporation of India). This is known as the "Order of Preference" or obligatory cession.

Furthermore, when seeking additional facultative or treaty capacity, insurers must follow a strict hierarchy: offering the risk first to GIC Re, then to Foreign Reinsurance Branches (FRBs) operating in India (like Swiss Re India, Munich Re India), and only then to Cross Border Reinsurers (CBRs) or the Lloyd's India market. This aims to maximize domestic retention of premium while maintaining solvency.

10. Summary & Formula Cheat-Sheet

  • Quota Share: Retained = \((1-\alpha)X\); Ceded = \(\alpha X\).
  • Surplus Cession %: \(\alpha = \max\left(0, \frac{SI - Retention}{SI}\right)\)
  • Excess of Loss Ceded: \( X_{ceded} = \min(\max(X - D, 0), L) \)
  • Burning Cost Rate: \( \frac{\text{Sum of Layer Losses}}{\text{Sum of GNPI}} \)
  • Rate on Line (RoL): \( \frac{\text{Premium}}{\text{Limit}} \)
  • Payback Period: \( \frac{1}{\text{RoL}} \) (Number of years for reinsurer to recoup a total limit loss).

Selected Solutions

Solution to 1: Ceded Premium = 30% of 10,000 = 3,000. Retained Premium = 7,000. Ceded Loss = 30% of 50,000 = 15,000. Retained Loss = 35,000.
Solution to 2: Retention = $500k. Surplus = $1.5M. Percentage ceded = 1.5M / 2.0M = 75%.
Solution to 3: Layer is $2M limit above $1M deductible. (a) Loss $500k < $1M -> Reinsurer pays 0. (b) Loss $1.5M -> Reinsurer pays $500k. (c) Loss $4M -> Reinsurer pays the max limit: $2M.
Solution to 5: Total Layer Losses = 0 + 2M + 5M + 1M + 0 = 8M. Total GNPI = 500M. Pure rate = 8/500 = 1.6%. Loaded rate = 1.6% * 1.5 = 2.4%.
Solution to 6: Retention = 1 Cr. Treaty Limit = 5 lines = 5 Cr. Total Treaty Capacity = 6 Cr. Sum Insured is 8 Cr. Therefore: Retained = 1 Cr (12.5%). Treaty Cession = 5 Cr (62.5%). Facultative Reinsurance needed = 2 Cr (25%).
Solution to 8: Initial Premium paid = $1M. Loss occurs, so reinstatement premium triggered. Reinstatement cost = 100% of initial = $1M. Total premium paid = $2M. (Insurer also bears the deductible on the loss).
