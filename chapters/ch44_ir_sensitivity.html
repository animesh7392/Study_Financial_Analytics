3. Intuition & Motivation

Imagine you are a balance sheet manager at Scottish Widows. You are staring at a £50 billion annuity portfolio. The liabilities are essentially a massive stream of fixed future cash outflows, stretching out over 50 years. To back these liabilities, you hold a mix of UK Gilts, corporate bonds, and interest rate swaps.

In September 2022, following the UK "mini-budget", Gilt yields spiked unprecedentedly. A 100-basis-point (1%) move in long-term yields used to take months; it happened in days. When yields rise, the present value of your liabilities plummets—which sounds great! However, the value of your fixed-rate assets plummets too. If your assets fall in value faster than your liabilities, your Own Funds (capital) shrinks, your Solvency II ratio drops, and suddenly, the PRA is on the phone asking if you need to suspend dividends.

Interest rate sensitivity analysis is the art and science of ensuring that when rates move—up, down, parallel, or twisted—the net impact on your capital remains manageable. We don't just ask "what if rates move 50 bps?" We ask, "what if the 10-year rate goes up by 100 bps, but the 30-year rate goes down by 50 bps?" Key Rate Duration (KRD) and sophisticated sensitivity matrices are our radar in the turbulent weather of financial markets.

4. Formal Theory

4.1 Duration Measures

Macaulay and Modified Duration

The Macaulay Duration (\(D_{Mac}\)) is the weighted average time to receive cash flows, weighted by their present value:

\[ D_{Mac} = \frac{1}{P} \sum_{t=1}^{T} t \times \frac{CF_t}{(1+y)^t} \]

Where \(P\) is the bond price, \(CF_t\) is the cash flow at time \(t\), and \(y\) is the yield to maturity.

The Modified Duration (\(D_{Mod}\)) measures the percentage price sensitivity to a change in yield:

\[ D_{Mod} = \frac{D_{Mac}}{1 + y/m} \]

Where \(m\) is the compounding frequency.

For bonds with embedded options (e.g., callable bonds), yield-to-maturity is not well-defined for shocks. We use Effective Duration (\(D_{Eff}\)):

\[ D_{Eff} = \frac{P_{-\Delta y} - P_{+\Delta y}}{2 \times P_0 \times \Delta y} \]

4.2 Convexity & Taylor Series Expansion

Duration-Convexity Approximation

Since the price-yield relationship is non-linear, Duration is only a linear approximation. To improve accuracy for larger rate shocks, we use the second derivative, Convexity (\(C\)):

\[ C = \frac{1}{P} \frac{\partial^2 P}{\partial y^2} = \frac{1}{P} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \frac{t(t+1)CF_t}{(1+y)^{t+2}} \]

The percentage change in price is given by the Taylor series expansion:

\[ \frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -D_{Mod}\Delta y + \frac{1}{2} C (\Delta y)^2 \]

4.3 Dollar Duration (DV01)

The Dollar Value of a 1 basis point (0.01%) move is called DV01 (or PV01).

\[ DV01 = P \times D_{Mod} \times 0.0001 \]

4.4 Key Rate Duration (KRD)

Parallel shifts are rare. The yield curve often twists or steepens. KRD measures sensitivity to a 1 bps shift at a specific tenor (e.g., 5-year, 10-year), holding other tenors constant.

\[ KRD_i = \frac{P(y_i - \Delta y_i) - P(y_i + \Delta y_i)}{2 \times P_0 \times \Delta y_i} \]

The total Effective Duration is the sum of the Key Rate Durations: \( D_{Eff} \approx \sum KRD_i \).

4.5 Balance Sheet Sensitivities (Solvency II)

For an insurer, Own Funds (\(OF\)) = Market Value of Assets (\(A\)) - Best Estimate Liabilities (\(L\)) - Risk Margin (\(RM\)).

The Solvency Ratio is \(SR = \frac{OF}{SCR}\).

\[ \Delta OF \approx (\Delta A) - (\Delta L) - (\Delta RM) \] \[ \Delta OF \approx - (A \cdot D_A - L \cdot D_L)\Delta y + \frac{1}{2}(A \cdot C_A - L \cdot C_L)(\Delta y)^2 \]
5. Worked Examples
Example 1: Balance Sheet ±100bp Sensitivity

Context: Scottish Widows has an asset portfolio worth £50,000m with \(D_A = 12.0\) and \(C_A = 200\). The corresponding liabilities are £46,000m with \(D_L = 13.5\) and \(C_L = 250\). SCR is £2,500m (assume invariant for this simple step). Base Own Funds = £4,000m. Base Solvency Ratio = 160%.

Shock: +100 bps (+1.00%) shift

  • \(\Delta A = -50000 \times 12 \times 0.01 + 0.5 \times 50000 \times 200 \times (0.01)^2 = -6000 + 500 = -£5,500m\)
  • New Asset Value = £44,500m
  • \(\Delta L = -46000 \times 13.5 \times 0.01 + 0.5 \times 46000 \times 250 \times (0.01)^2 = -6210 + 575 = -£5,635m\)
  • New Liability Value = £40,365m
  • New Own Funds = 44500 - 40365 = £4,135m
  • New Solvency Ratio = 4135 / 2500 = 165.4%

Insight: The duration gap (\(12.0\) vs \(13.5\)) means liabilities fall faster than assets when rates rise, increasing Own Funds.

Example 2: Reverse Stress Testing the Solvency Ratio

Context: Using the data above, the Board's risk appetite sets a red threshold if the Solvency Ratio breaches 130%. SCR is £2,500m. Thus, breach happens if Own Funds fall to \(1.30 \times 2500 = £3,250m\).

We need \(\Delta OF = 3250 - 4000 = -£750m\).

Ignoring convexity for a moment to solve for \(\Delta y\):

\(\Delta OF \approx -(A \cdot D_A - L \cdot D_L) \Delta y\)

\(-750 = - (50000 \times 12 - 46000 \times 13.5) \Delta y\)

\(-750 = - (600000 - 621000) \Delta y = 21000 \Delta y\)

\(\Delta y = -750 / 21000 = -0.0357\) or -357 basis points.

Conclusion: A massive yield drop of 357 bps is required to breach the 130% threshold. The balance sheet is well protected against falling rates.

Example 3: KRD Profile for a Gilt Portfolio

Consider a simplified portfolio of three Gilts: 5Y, 10Y, and 20Y. We measure sensitivities to specific tenor shocks.

Portfolio KRD Calculation:

  • Total Value = £450m
  • Port 5Y KRD = \([100(4.5) + 200(0.8) + 150(0.5)] / 450 = 685 / 450 = 1.52\)
  • Port 10Y KRD = \([100(0) + 200(8.2) + 150(2.0)] / 450 = 1940 / 450 = 4.31\)
  • Port 20Y KRD = \([100(0) + 200(0) + 150(13.5)] / 450 = 2025 / 450 = 4.50\)

If the yield curve steepens (5Y rate -50bps, 20Y rate +50bps), the portfolio price change is:

\(\Delta P / P \approx -(1.52 \times -0.0050) - (4.31 \times 0) - (4.50 \times +0.0050) = 0.0076 - 0.0225 = -0.0149\) (-1.49%).

6. Python Code: KRD and Sensitivities

This script computes bond price, duration, convexity, and evaluates price changes using the Taylor approximation.


import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

def bond_pricer(fv, coupon_rate, ytm, periods, freq=1):
    """Calculates bond price, modified duration, and convexity"""
    c = fv * coupon_rate / freq
    y = ytm / freq
    n = periods * freq
    
    t = np.arange(1, n + 1)
    cash_flows = np.full(n, c)
    cash_flows[-1] += fv
    
    discount_factors = 1 / (1 + y)**t
    pv_cf = cash_flows * discount_factors
    price = np.sum(pv_cf)
    
    # Macaulay Duration
    mac_dur = np.sum(t * pv_cf) / price / freq
    mod_dur = mac_dur / (1 + y)
    
    # Convexity
    convexity = np.sum(t * (t + 1) * pv_cf) / (price * (1 + y)**2 * freq**2)
    
    return price, mod_dur, convexity

# Scenario Analysis
shocks = np.arange(-0.02, 0.025, 0.005) # -200bps to +200bps
results = []

fv = 100
coupon = 0.04
ytm_base = 0.05
years = 20

p0, d_mod, conv = bond_pricer(fv, coupon, ytm_base, years)

for shift in shocks:
    ytm_new = ytm_base + shift
    p_exact, _, _ = bond_pricer(fv, coupon, ytm_new, years)
    
    # Taylor Approximation
    dp_linear = -d_mod * shift
    dp_convex = 0.5 * conv * shift**2
    p_approx = p0 * (1 + dp_linear + dp_convex)
    
    results.append({
        'Shift (bps)': shift * 10000,
        'Exact Price': p_exact,
        'Approx Price': p_approx,
        'Linear Only': p0 * (1 + dp_linear)
    })

df = pd.DataFrame(results)
print(df.loc[df['Shift (bps)'].isin([-100, 0, 100])])

7. Excel Implementation: Building the Sensitivity Dashboard

Building a robust IR sensitivity dashboard in Excel is a core competency for actuaries. Here is the step-by-step setup.

Step 1: The Input & Base Case Setup

Create a block in range A1:B6:

  • A1: Base Asset Value (£m) | B1: 50000
  • A2: Asset Mod Duration | B2: 12.0
  • A3: Asset Convexity | B3: 200
  • A4: Base Liab Value (£m) | B4: 46000
  • A5: Liab Mod Duration | B5: 13.5
  • A6: Liab Convexity | B6: 250

Step 2: Base Balance Sheet Metrics

Calculate base outputs in A8:B10:

  • A8: SCR (£m) | B8: 2500 (Hardcoded for this example)
  • A9: Base Own Funds (£m) | B9: =B1-B4 (Yields 4000)
  • A10: Base Solvency Ratio | B10: =B9/B8 (Yields 160%, format as %)

Step 3: Building the Sensitivities using a Data Table

We want a table showing impacts from -100 bps to +100 bps.

1. In cell D1, input a placeholder shock: 0.00 (This is our dummy cell for the Data Table).

2. In D3:I3, setup the headers for the calculation formulas:

  • D3: Shift (bps) (Leave blank, will be row headers)
  • E3: Asset Value | Formula: =B1*(1 + (-B2*$D$1) + (0.5*B3*($D$1^2)))
  • F3: Liability Value | Formula: =B4*(1 + (-B5*$D$1) + (0.5*B6*($D$1^2)))
  • G3: Own Funds | Formula: =E3-F3
  • H3: Solvency Ratio | Formula: =G3/B8
  • I3: Dividend Capacity | Formula: =IF(H3>1.5, (H3-1.5)*B8, 0) (Assumes target SR is 150%)

3. In D4:D12, input the shocks: -0.0100, -0.0075, -0.0050, -0.0025, 0.0000, 0.0025, 0.0050, 0.0075, 0.0100.

4. Creating the Data Table:

  • Highlight the range D3:I12.
  • Go to Data -> What-If Analysis -> Data Table.
  • Row input cell: Leave blank.
  • Column input cell: Select $D$1.
  • Click OK. Excel calculates the entire sensitivity matrix instantly.

Step 4: Conditional Formatting (Traffic Lights)

Select the Solvency Ratio outputs in H4:H12.

  • Go to Home -> Conditional Formatting -> New Rule.
  • Format style: 3-Color Scale.
  • Type: Number for all.
  • Minimum: Value 1.30 (Color Red)
  • Midpoint: Value 1.40 (Color Yellow/Amber)
  • Maximum: Value 1.50 (Color Green)

This provides an immediate visual risk dashboard for the ALM committee.

8. Common Mistakes & Misconceptions
  • Ignoring Convexity for Large Shocks: Duration is a linear tangent. For a 10 bps move, duration is fine. For a 100 bps move, ignoring convexity can misstate the balance sheet by hundreds of millions of pounds.
  • Assuming Parallel Shifts: Yield curves rarely move in perfect parallel. Relying solely on Effective Duration while ignoring Key Rate Durations masks curve twist risks.
  • Confusing Macaulay and Modified Duration: Macaulay is in units of years; Modified is a percentage sensitivity. Using Macaulay where Modified is required in the Taylor formula will give disastrously wrong risk metrics.
  • Static SCR Assumption: In reality, as rates move, the Solvency Capital Requirement (SCR) also changes (due to changes in spread risk, market risk modules, and risk margin). Our examples hold SCR constant for simplicity, but a full internal model must recalculate SCR dynamically.
9. Industry Application: The 2022 UK LDI Crisis

In late September 2022, following the UK government's "mini-budget", 30-year Gilt yields spiked by over 100 basis points in a matter of days. This triggered collateral calls in Liability Driven Investment (LDI) funds used by UK pension schemes.

For life insurers like Scottish Widows and Lloyds Banking Group, the impact was profound. While the Solvency II capital position often improves when rates rise (because the duration of liabilities usually exceeds the duration of assets), the liquidity strain from margin calls on interest rate swaps was extreme.

This event fundamentally shifted PRA stress testing (such as the ACS - Annual Cyclical Scenario). Insurers must now run severe reverse stress tests specifically focusing on liquidity exhaustion during violent curve steepening and parallel upward shifts. Key Rate Duration at the 20Y and 30Y tenors became the most critical metric on the ALM dashboard.

10. Summary & Formula Cheat-Sheet

Core Formulas:

  • Modified Duration: \( D_{Mod} = \frac{D_{Mac}}{1+y} \)
  • Effective Duration: \( D_{Eff} = \frac{P_{-\Delta y} - P_{+\Delta y}}{2 P_0 \Delta y} \)
  • Price Approximation: \( \Delta P \approx P_0 \times [ -D_{Mod}\Delta y + \frac{1}{2} C (\Delta y)^2 ] \)
  • Dollar Duration (DV01): \( DV01 = P_0 \times D_{Mod} \times 0.0001 \)
  • Own Funds Impact: \( \Delta OF \approx -(A \cdot D_A - L \cdot D_L)\Delta y \) (ignoring convexity)
